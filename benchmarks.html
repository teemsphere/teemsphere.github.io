Appendix A — Solver benchmarks

This appendix summarises the measurements behind the method recommendations in Solving the model. They show how solve time and memory change with model size and matrix method, first on a laptop-sized machine and then for models of up to 284 million equations on a server. The block methods follow Van Ha and Kompas (2016), who introduced "SBBD" on an intertemporal regional model of up to 32.7 million variables, Van Ha et al. (2017), who applied it to an intertemporal GTAP model of 108.5 million equations, and Kompas and Van Ha (2019), whose nested method solved an intertemporal GTAP model of 112 regions, 57 commodities and 47 periods, more than 500 million equations; see References.

A.1 How the runs were made

  • Models: GTAPv7 (static) and GTAP-RE (intertemporal) on the GTAP 12 database, at the aggregations listed in each table, with all endowments. Static models were run both as the full system (ignore_condense = TRUE) and condensed (the vetted default).
  • Shock and method: aoall +20 % everywhere, solved with Gragg 2-4-8.
  • Machine: a 12 GB memory limit with 4 or 8 cores, emulating a 16 GB laptop running Docker Desktop. The tables show the 8-core runs, with resources written as tasks × threads. The large models ran on the full server: 32 cores and 125 GB.
  • Reading the times: the runs used server processors, so absolute times will differ on your machine; the ratios between methods carry over. Each configuration ran once.
  • Solver: 1.1.0 development builds, September 2026.

A.2 Intertemporal models

GTAP-RE, 8 cores; aggregations are REG × ACTS × periods. Resources are written tasks × threads, and “Best SBBD” is the fastest split of the 8 cores.

Aggregation Equations LU SBBD 1 × 8 Best SBBD Memory
big3 × full × 6 1.30M 177 s 52 s 34 s (8 × 1) 0.9 GB
big3 × full × 11 2.38M 435 s 96 s 60 s (8 × 1) 1.5 GB
big3 × full × 21 4.54M – 189 s 106 s (4 × 2) 2.1 GB
WB7 × full × 11 5.87M – 334 s 173 s (8 × 1) 3.2 GB
big3 × full × 41 8.85M – 373 s 215 s (8 × 1) 4.5 GB
  • SBBD is 5 to 7 times faster than LU.
  • Tasks beat threads: splitting 8 cores into 4 or 8 tasks is 35 to 50 % faster than 1 task with 8 threads, and 8 tasks gain at most about 10 % over 4.
  • Memory is modest; every intertemporal run fitted comfortably in 12 GB.

A.3 Static models, full system

GTAPv7 with ignore_condense = TRUE, 8 cores; aggregations are REG × ACTS. LU uses 8 threads, and “Best DBBD” is the fastest split of the 8 cores.

Aggregation Equations LU Best DBBD DBBD / LU
WB23 × energy 346k 36 s 17 s (4 × 2) 0.46
WB7 × full 534k 60 s 29 s (4 × 2) 0.48
R32 × medium 1.41M 470 s 170 s (8 × 1) 0.36
R32 × full 3.46M 2,618 s 564 s (4 × 2) 0.22
medium × medium 3.77M 3,095 s 789 s (2 × 4) 0.26
large × medium 7.69M not finished in 3 h 2,373 s (2 × 4) –
  • DBBD is faster than LU at every size, and the advantage grows with size.
  • LU gains almost nothing from threads on the full system.

A.4 Static models, condensed

The same aggregations with the vetted condensation, 8 cores. Condensation cuts the system about twentyfold.

Aggregation Equations LU 1 × 8 DBBD 2 × 4
WB23 × energy 20k 14 s 23 s
WB7 × full 25k 31 s 60 s
R32 × medium 70k 125 s 153 s
R32 × full 110k 456 s 511 s
medium × medium 140k 713 s 623 s
large × medium 230k not finished in 3 h out of memory
  • Threaded LU is the faster choice up to about 100 thousand condensed equations; DBBD takes over above that.
  • Condensed DBBD gets slower with more tasks, and needs more memory than on the full system.
  • The largest model solves faster uncondensed with DBBD (2,373 s above) than condensed with either method.

A.5 Large models

A server with 32 cores (AMD EPYC 7763) and 125 GB of memory, with no container limit. All runs used Johansen except the first, which used Gragg 2-4-8. GTAP-INT is a research model that is not shipped with teems.

Model Aggregation Equations Method Time Memory
GTAP-RE WB7 × full × 41 21.9M SBBD 8 × 8 422 s 10 GB
GTAPv7 (full) full × full 40.5M DBBD 8 × 1 1,449 s 87 GB
GTAP-RE WB7 × full × 430 230M SBBD 8 × 4 417 s 91 GB
GTAP-RE WB7 × full × 468 250M SBBD 8 × 1 690 s 100 GB
GTAP-INT – 234M NDBBD 1 × 32 13,715 s 130 GB
GTAP-INT – 284M NDBBD 1 × 8 21,135 s 132 GB
  • SBBD solves intertemporal models of 250 million equations in minutes, as long as they fit in memory.
  • For the same 40.5M-equation GTAPv7 system, an earlier build took 27 hours with LU on the full system and 2.9 hours with LU on the condensed model, against 24 minutes with DBBD above.
  • NDBBD is much slower than SBBD, but it solves intertemporal models that SBBD cannot fit: on the 234M-equation GTAP-INT model, SBBD ran out of memory. The largest model solved, at 284M equations, used nearly all of the server’s memory.
  • Adding tasks raises memory: DBBD on 16 tasks ran out of memory on the 40.5M-equation system that 8 tasks solved.

A.6 Memory

Peak memory grows with the size of the full system, even when the model is condensed, because the data arrays follow the full model.

Method Peak memory per million equations of the full system
LU 0.6 to 0.9 GB
DBBD 0.85 GB + 0.27 GB per task
SBBD 0.36 GB + 0.02 GB per task

A condensed model needs about the same memory as its full system under LU, and 1.3 to 1.55 times as much under DBBD.

Within 12 GB:

  • intertemporal models fitted at every size measured (up to 8.85M equations; the model above suggests about 20M);
  • full static systems fitted to 7.7M equations with LU or DBBD on 2 tasks; DBBD on 4 or more tasks ran out of memory at that size;
  • condensed static systems fitted at every size with LU; DBBD on 8 tasks ran out of memory from 110 thousand condensed equations.

ems_solve() uses these estimates to stop a run that will not fit before it starts (see Memory).
